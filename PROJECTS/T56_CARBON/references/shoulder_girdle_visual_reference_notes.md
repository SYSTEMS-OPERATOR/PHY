## High-value topology cues

The plates suggest the following abstract mechanical ideas:

1. **Central segmented dorsal datum**  
   A repeating centerline assembly organizes the upper torso and gives bilateral shoulder structures a common reference.

2. **Mobile scapular structures**  
   The large bilateral posterior plates read as moving carriages or yokes rather than fixed ribs.

3. **Anterior-superior control links**  
   Compact links near the upper chest appear to guide or share the motion of the outboard shoulder structures.

4. **Outboard removable shoulder modules**  
   The spherical shoulder assemblies are visually distinct from both torso and humeral members, supporting a replaceable-cartridge interpretation.

5. **Separate structural and contour layers**  
   Inner mechanisms, plate-like structural members, and outer shells appear separable. This supports the T-5.6 nested-thorax doctrine.

6. **Open truss long members**  
   Upper-arm and forearm members use low-mass open geometry with reinforced joint ends.

7. **Protected centerline routing**  
   The spine and thoracic center imply a protected corridor for structure, wiring, and service access.

## T-5.6 translation

The project adopts only the topology:

- central thoracic root
- bilateral moving scapular carriages
- clavicular guidance
- outboard shoulder cartridges
- serviceable layered construction
- open long-member language

The project deliberately rejects or softens:

- armored shoulder breadth
- oversized angular chest shells
- exposed telescoping mechanisms
- sharp plate edges
- ornamental surface patterns as structural requirements
- direct use of visible proportions or part outlines

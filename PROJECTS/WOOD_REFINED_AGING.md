# WOOD REFINED AGING

Offline reference for how candidate woods age after they are cut, carved, drilled, sanded, pinned, sealed, handled, and placed into service as PHY armature components.

## Core conclusion

Tree age does not directly predict refined-material endurance.

After refinement, endurance is governed by:

- moisture exchange
- dimensional movement
- end-grain sealing
- finish behavior
- UV exposure
- biological decay risk
- extractive bleed
- hardware staining
- drill and bushing stress concentrations
- grain continuity through small parts
- storage and service environment

Older or slower-grown material may carry useful endurance traits, but those traits must survive the transition from living tree or dead standing wood into shaped component stock.

## Universal rules

Wood remains hygroscopic. It continues to exchange moisture with surrounding air after cutting and finishing.

Outdoor weathering begins at the surface and is driven by UV radiation, moisture, temperature change, freeze-thaw, abrasion, and microorganisms.

Wood surface lignin can photodegrade under UV exposure, which changes surface chemistry and can reduce finish adhesion.

Clear water-repellent finishes should be treated as renewable maintenance layers, not permanent armor.

Extractive-rich woods may bleed through finishes. Tannin-rich woods may stain around reactive metal contact.

## Project hypothesis

Extreme tree age correlates with survival traits, not guaranteed engineering strength.

After refinement into components, endurance depends on whether those traits translate into stable, decay-resistant, finish-compatible, hardware-compatible, moisture-tolerant artifact behavior.

Therefore:

- age is evidence, not a material property
- density is a material property
- shrinkage is a material property
- decay resistance is a material property
- workability is a fabrication behavior
- legal provenance is a gate
- sample testing is required before final component cuts

## Immediate design choice

Coast redwood remains the strongest current build candidate for final visible components.

Bald cypress and western juniper remain useful endurance challengers.

Great Basin bristlecone pine remains the age/endurance control case, not authorized cut stock.

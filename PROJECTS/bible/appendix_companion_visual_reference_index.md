# Appendix G3 — Companion Visual Reference Index

## G3.1 Purpose

This appendix curates high-quality visual references for the Wooden Companion and Wooden Skeleton precedent sections.

The goal is not decoration. The goal is visual design evidence for:

- posture
- carved presence
- visible joints
- hollow-body strategy
- removable panels
- service access
- puppet head and hand mechanics
- automata mechanisms
- surface aging
- repair logic
- pose-holding logic
- sensor-ready embodiment analogues

## G3.2 Completion status

This appendix is justified and should be included in the Wooden Armature Bible.

Current status:

- complete as a high-level visual-reference scaffold
- not yet complete as a final image bibliography
- direct museum/object links still need a source-hardening pass
- images must remain high quality, traceable, and relevant to armature/body construction

## G3.3 Inclusion rules

Accept:

- museum collection pages
- museum conservation pages
- official museum articles with high-quality images
- academic object records
- high-resolution public-domain or open-license repository pages
- Wikimedia Commons only when the image is clear, traceable, and not lower quality than museum alternatives
- mechanism photographs and cutaway views
- restoration/conservation images

Reject:

- low-resolution blog thumbnails
- AI-generated illustrations
- unsourced Pinterest-style images
- generic mannequin images
- decorative photos with no construction relevance
- images where the object cannot be identified
- images that show beauty but no useful structure, mechanism, surface, or fabrication lesson

## G3.4 Image-quality tags

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| HQ-OFFICIAL | official museum/source image, high quality |
| HQ-COMMONS | high-quality Commons/open image with traceable object |
| MECHANISM | visible mechanism, joint, cavity, or moving system |
| SURFACE | useful surface/finish/aging reference |
| PRESENCE | strong body-presence reference |
| ARMATURE | useful for joint, pose, support, or skeleton logic |
| NEEDS-LINK | object identified, direct best image link still needed |
| REJECT | known but not useful enough for this appendix |

## G3.6 High-priority image entries to harden first

### A'a from Rurutu

Why first:

- hollow anthropomorphic body
- removable back panel
- internal cavity
- surface-carved smaller figures
- directly supports hollow-body, sensor-bay, and memory-chamber doctrine

Desired source:

- British Museum official object page and associated high-quality images

### Artist lay figures

Why first:

- closest historical companion to a poseable wooden studio body
- direct joint and pose-holding relevance
- bridges mannequin, armature, and companion lines

Desired source:

- museum collection pages or `Silent Partners` exhibition documentation

### Bunraku puppet heads / hands

Why first:

- shows that head, gaze, and hands carry disproportionate presence
- construction and service logic are directly relevant

Desired source:

- Japanese museum or theatre educational sources with mechanism images

### Karakuri ningyo

Why first:

- hidden mechanism and meaningful gesture loops
- strongest historical link to simple wooden/wood-adjacent automaton behavior

Desired source:

- museum pages or Karakuri Zui scans/scholarly editions

### Tipu's Tiger

Why first:

- wooden shell containing sound and mechanism
- excellent precedent for hollow carved body + internal system + service panels

Desired source:

- Victoria and Albert Museum object and conservation pages

### Jaquet-Droz automata

Why first:

- hand/gaze coordination
- mechanical memory
- serviceable mechanism in companion-like figure

Desired source:

- Musée d'art et d'histoire de Neuchâtel official media

## G3.8 Current conclusion

There is enough historical visual material to justify a dedicated image-link appendix.

The first visual set should focus on construction and armature relevance, not general beauty:

- hollow bodies
- removable panels
- visible joints
- puppet heads and hands
- automata mechanisms
- poseable lay figures
- BJD socket/stringing systems
- anatomical reference figures

Images should be treated as design evidence only when paired with source and interpretation.
